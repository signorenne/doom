#+title:    :private butler
#+subtitle: Hudson, at your service

* Description
A butler who can be summoned with ~SPC h j~. He appears in a bubble beside
your work, in a child frame in the GUI and a popup at the bottom in a
terminal, and says something worth hearing:

- a hint for the mode you are in, taken from the cheat sheet, so it is
  always a key this config really has;
- a hint from anywhere in the sheet;
- an answer: ask him in your words (~a~ in his menu), he looks the words
  up in the sheet and the READMEs and answers with the line that fits
  best, the key when it is one, and where it says so; when the lines
  beside it under the same section answer as well he gives them too, up
  to three, so a question about dividing a frame comes back with both
  of the keys that do it; when two sections fit as well he asks
  which you meant; ~o~ opens the docs at the line. When the words leave
  it open he says the best answer and offers the rest in a menu of his
  own, numbered, each with where it comes from, so a question with
  more than one reading is settled by pressing a number rather than by
  him guessing; ~c~ there puts it to Claude instead. A key is an answer
  one can act on, so lines of prose stand aside when two of the hits
  carry one.
  When the words alone do not settle it, or when asked with ~C-u~, or on
  ~A~ for a question you know to be a hard one, he puts it to Claude
  through gptel with the lines he found, so the answer is a sentence;
  gptel wants a key for the Anthropic API, and when there is none he
  goes through ~+butler-claude-program~, the Claude Code command line,
  which is signed in already: no key to set up, and no tools, since he
  wants an answer and not an errand. Asked about this configuration he
  answers from its docs and may not invent a key; asked about anything
  else he answers from what he knows, as a well read butler would. He is
  told which part of the docs the buffer he was called from belongs to,
  since a key means one thing in a C++ buffer and another in an Org
  document, and he says where a key from elsewhere works;
  ~r~ puts the last question to Claude when what the docs said was not
  what you meant. The docs stay the source either way, so he cannot
  make a key up. He calls you what ~+butler-address~ says and never a
  name: not the login name, not the one on the commits, however he is
  pressed for it;
- a word of his own, from ~+butler-quips~;
- the greeting of the hour, the same one the dashboard uses.

He also stands on the dashboard, under the banner, holding the greeting of
the hour in his bubble: his module sets ui's ~+ui-dashboard-greeting-function~
and joins ~+ui-dashboard-tick-hook~, so the dashboard draws him without
knowing him. There and when summoned he is alive, on three
tracks at once: which way the head is turned, left, middle or right; what
the face is doing, a blink, a wink, a smile, wide eyes, a sigh; and what
the body is doing, straightening the tie or a shrug. Any face and any body
move can happen in any head position, because the figure is composed from
the three each time it is drawn. A turn stays until the next turn, so he
will blink, smile and fix his tie looking to one side, then look about and
carry on in the middle. Nothing moves up or down.

The faces are ~+butler-faces~ and the bodies ~+butler-bodies~, rows of the
art swapped in; the gestures ~+butler-gestures~, each a weight and a list
of steps, a step being which track, which value, and how long to hold it
in tenths of a second. He never makes the same gesture twice running,
every hold is stretched a little at random, and ~+butler-idle-ticks~ is
the range he keeps still in between.

He stays once summoned, animated, while you work, and goes when told:
~x~ in his menu, ~SPC h J~, or ~+butler/dismiss~. His menu opens with
him and closes with ~q~ or ~ESC~, leaving him where he is; ~SPC h j~
while he is there opens it again: ~h~ another hint for this mode, ~n~ one
from anywhere, ~s~ a word of his own, ~g~ the greeting, ~c~ copy what he
said.

Where he stands is chosen from the same menu, laid out like a keypad: ~7~
and ~9~ the top corners, ~1~ and ~3~ the bottom ones, ~5~ the middle, ~0~
just below the cursor, ~m~ the next of ~+butler-positions~. The arrow keys
move him ~+butler-nudge-step~ pixels from wherever he is, and the mouse
drags him: press on him, move, let go; a press that does not move him
changes nothing. A named corner keeps clear of his menu while it is up
and is taken again once his own menu closes, another menu closing is none
of his business; a spot he was nudged or dragged to is his own. He is
kept inside the frame either way, placed again when the frame changes
size, and the spot is remembered by savehist, so he is found where he was
left next session.

The child frame he floats in has its focus redirected to the frame he
stands in; without that, a mouse press on him is an event from another
frame, which Emacs turns into a switch-frame that selects his frame and
suspends any menu. That was why dragging did nothing and closed the menu.

A long answer does not make a long butler. The bubble says
~+butler-bubble-max-lines~ at the most, and never more than the frame can
hold; past that the last line reads =... (R reads it all)=, and ~R~ opens
the whole of it in =*butler says*=, an ordinary buffer that scrolls and
searches, while ~c~ copies it whole. What he said is kept entire either
way; only the bubble is short.

The bubble is as wide as what it holds, ~+butler-bubble-width~ at the
widest and ~+butler-bubble-min-width~ at the narrowest, and no taller
than what it holds either: a brief word leaves no empty room inside it,
and a bubble shorter than he is stands against his middle. He keeps his
own height whatever he says, so the block keeps its shape from one thing he says to the next
and only grows for a long answer; where what he says came from, the
section of the sheet or Claude, stands in its corner in the shadow. The
child frame is snapped to its corner by what it actually measures, the
size worked out in characters being a pixel or two out under a
fractional scale, which at a corner shows as a gap between him and the
edge. It is given that size in characters rather than asked for it:
after a resize a child frame reports its old size for a while, and
placing him by that left a gap under him or cut a corner off. The popup
keeps ~+butler-margin~ of blank space round him and the bubble, the same
on every side, and none by default: the drawing carries no spare column
either, so in a corner he stands against the edge of it rather than a
step inside.

Where he stands is written down as soon as he stops moving: savehist
keeps it, but Doom has savehist write only when Emacs is closed
properly, and a session that ends any other way would lose the spot.

On the dashboard he does not say the one thing all session: every
~+butler-dashboard-say-ticks~ (250, so about twenty-five seconds) he says
something else, the greeting of the hour, a word of his own or a hint
from the sheet. Only what fits the bubble as it stands is taken, and
the block is replaced in place, so the dashboard never shifts under it.
Nil there and he says the one thing, as before.

He is named after Angus Hudson of Upstairs, Downstairs. ~+butler-name~
changes that; Carson, Jeeves and Brabinger have all served.

* Keybindings
| Key     | Command         | What it does                 |
|---------+-----------------+------------------------------|
| ~SPC h j~ | ~+butler/summon~  | he comes, and his menu opens |
| ~a~       | ~+butler/ask~     | ask him, in your words; ~C-u~ asks Claude |
| ~A~       | ~+butler/ask-claude~ | ask, and have Claude answer from the docs |
| ~r~       | ~+butler/ask-again~ | the last question again, to Claude |
| ~1~ .. ~5~  | ~+butler/choose~  | in his choice menu, the answer you meant |
| ~R~       | ~+butler/read~    | the whole of what he said, in a buffer |
| ~o~       | ~+butler/show-source~ | the docs where the answer came from |
| ~q~       |                 | the menu closes, he stays; so does any command of it |
| ~x~       | ~+butler/dismiss~ | he goes                      |
| ~SPC h J~ | ~+butler/dismiss~ | he goes, without the menu    |
| ~7 9 1 3~ |                 | a corner                     |
| ~5~ ~0~     |                 | the middle, below the cursor |
| arrows  |                 | a step; the menu stays open for the next |
| mouse   | ~+butler/drag~    | drag him; dropped in a corner he takes it |

* Configuration
- ~+butler-name~, ~+butler-art~, ~+butler-quips~.
- ~+butler-claude-program~ ("claude") and ~+butler-claude-seconds~ (120):
  how he reaches Claude without a key, and how long he waits for it.
- ~+butler-address~ (="sir"=): how he calls you, in the greeting, in
  his quips and in every word he has for you. A form of address rather
  than a name, since a butler does not use his employer's.
- ~+butler-greetings~: what he says on the dashboard by the part of the
  day; the first line of each takes ~+butler-address~.
- ~+butler-bubble-width~ (44) and ~+butler-bubble-min-width~ (22), how wide
  the bubble may be; ~+butler-bubble-max-lines~ (12), how much it says
  before ~R~ takes over.
- ~+butler-margin~ (0 . 0), ~+butler-positions~, ~+butler-nudge-step~ (60).
- The hints come from the docs module, ~+docs-cheatsheet-tips~ and
  ~+docs-cheatsheet-section-for~; =doom doctor= says so when docs or ui
  is off, and =packages.el= declares posframe, the child frame.
- The files: =autoload/butler.el= is what he knows, how he is drawn and
  moved, and where he stands; =autoload/menu.el= the commands and his
  menu; ~+butler--advance~ is the one clock, ticked by the dashboard's
  hook there and by his own timer when summoned.
